If you go tomorrow: where to start

Scores are relative (0–100) among 5,421 valid crossings of the mapped Nada/Renfro contact. They are not probabilities. Coordinates are WGS84 to 4 decimals (~10 m), but the 1:24,000 contact itself is only good to roughly ±15–40 m, so treat each point as "the contact is within a few tens of metres of here, in this channel." Land access was not evaluated — nearly all of this is private land or Daniel Boone National Forest; get permission and check rules before collecting.

How to walk a target: drive or hike to the walk-start point (the downstream end of the solid primary reach), then walk upstream along the channel checking gravel bars, riffle heads, pool tails and bank toes, until you reach the source crossing where the channel cuts through the Renfro ledge. The thick translucent band just below the crossing is the source zone, where float is least travelled and least diluted. If the primary reach is barren, the dotted extended reach continues downstream.

Day 1 — Big Hill cluster (Madison / Rockcastle / Jackson)

This is the best-supported area. The Bighill, Berea and Johnetta GQ maps draw the Renfro separately above the Nada, so the line is the real target contact, not an interpolation. It is also the only place with a published technical description of silica (chalcedony + quartz) geodes replacing the upper Nada dolostone (KSPG 2013 Stop 6B, US-421 roadcut). Dever (1999) measured a thick Renfro (16.7–17.5 m) here with a 1.5–5 m cherty, geode-bearing basal dolomite (sections 83 Bighill, 87 Owsley Fork).

Day 2 — Middle Fork Station Camp Creek and Red Lick / Horns River (Estill / Jackson)

Along and east of the projected Locust Branch Fault trend the Renfro is thinner (6.3–9.7 m, Dever 1999), but it still sits directly on Nada shale, and Dever reports brecciated dolomite with quartz bodies as far northeast as Drip Rock (section 95). This is also where independent collector reports cluster (Station Camp Creek, South Fork, Rock Lick Creek, Middle Fork side streams). Those reports were not used in scoring, but the geology-only ranking puts Middle Fork side streams near the top anyway.

If you prefer the Irvine / Powell side

Rank 3 (37.6333, -83.8883, Irvine quad, unnamed hollow → Buck Creek; walk start 37.6378, -83.8804) and rank 9 (37.7654, -83.8586, Stanton quad, Powell County, → Hardwick Creek) score well on terrain and source supply. On these quadrangles the Renfro and Nada are mapped as one unit (Mbrn), though, so the true contact lies a few feet to about 10 m below the drawn line. Search the source zone and the slope just below the Renfro ledge, not just the exact line.

What makes these better than the other valid crossings

The top 120 have a median of 25–31 km of target contact upstream of the reach end, against 17 km for unranked valid crossings. They also have deeper incision (28–33 m vs 21 m) and higher source concentration. The rank-1 to rank-10 group combines all of these:

  1. The contact is verified from the GQ legend (Nada mapped separately under the Renfro in 9 of the top 10).
  2. A lot of that contact drains into a short reach, with little Pennsylvanian sandstone to dilute it.
  3. Steep, deeply incised hollows keep eroding the Nada slope just below the Renfro ledge.
  4. Moderate 4–12% reach gradients, where gravel bars should hold nodules without them washing through.
  5. They sit in the facies belt where the lower Renfro/upper Nada is documented to carry silica geodes.

Collecting history was not the reason for any rank. Documented localities were used only to check the ranking afterwards (see AUDITS.md).
