Why Jackson, Lee, Estill, Madison, Powell and Rockcastle?

Short answer

Kentucky agate turns up where three conditions overlap. The first is always present in the belt; the second and third are what actually limit it.

  1. The host horizon is at the surface and being cut by streams. The uppermost Borden Formation (Nada Member, or its southern equivalent the Wildie Member) lies directly beneath the Renfro dolostone. This outcrop band follows the eroded edge of the Mississippian carbonate bench (the Knobs / Pottsville Escarpment front) from Rockcastle County northeast through Madison, Jackson, Estill, Lee and Powell into Menifee.
  2. The right Renfro/upper-Nada facies. Silica (chalcedony and quartz) replaces dolostone and evaporite nodules only where the lower Renfro/upper Nada was a restricted, peritidal, evaporite-bearing dolomite. KSPG 2013 Stop 6B at Big Hill describes "randomly arranged silica (chalcedony and quartz) geodes that appear to replace dolostone" in the upper Nada/Wildie dolostone bed. Dever (1999) shows the basal Renfro is 16.7–17.5 m thick with a 1.5–5 m cherty, geode-bearing dolomite west of the projected Locust Branch Fault trend (sections 83, 87). East of that trend it thins to 6.3–9.7 m (sections 88–95), and brecciated dolomite with quartz bodies is last reported at Drip Rock (section 95).
  3. Terrain that erodes the horizon quickly into small, undiluted channels. Inside the KGS "general location of Kentucky agates" outline, the hillslopes along the contact are much steeper: the median erosion index E is 0.74 inside against 0.40 outside. Many short hollows head right at the Renfro ledge.

What changes outside the belt

  • Southwest (Rockcastle → Pulaski/Lincoln): the Nada splits into the Wildie and Halls Gap Members, and farther south the Wildie pinches out. On the Brodhead, Crab Orchard, Maretburg, Mount Vernon and Woodstock quadrangles the Renfro rests on the Halls Gap (Mbh), so the upper-Nada host is missing or not mapped. Quartz geodes there come from other horizons: Muldraugh/Salem-Warsaw carbonates (Dever sections 74, 75) and Renfro-type-area geodes. These are classed AMBIGUOUS or NOT TARGET and not used.
  • Northeast (Menifee, Rowan): the Nada continues, mapped with the Renfro as one unit (Mbrn) on the Frenchburg, Means, Scranton and Zachariah GQs. The Renfro becomes thin (about 5–10 ft in Powell County per the Stanton, Slade and Pomeroyton legends), and Dever reports cherty/geodiferous dolomite and quartz bodies fading out northeast of Drip Rock. The host is present but the silica-replacement facies apparently weakens.
  • North and west (Bluegrass): the Borden has been eroded away, or crops out only as older members below the Nada.
  • East and south (Cumberland Plateau): the horizon is buried under Pennsylvanian rocks.

Which variable is most restrictive?

Facies of the lower Renfro/upper Nada, meaning evaporite-bearing dolomite that silica could replace. This is more restrictive than outcrop. The outcrop band of the target contact runs about 100 km from Rockcastle to Menifee, with about 2,540 km of mapped contact line (519 km verified as a separately mapped Nada/Renfro contact, 1,815 km as the top of the combined unit, 210 km probable Wildie), but documented and reported silica is limited to the Big Hill – Drip Rock – Irvine – Powell segment. Terrain is second: the steep, incised hollows inside the KGS outline both supply and expose float.

Is the six-county belt partly a collecting-history artifact?

Partly, and the test is built into the data. With the regional-evidence input R (the KGS outline, 20% of the score) removed, 91 of the top 120 targets stay the same and 7 of the top 10 stay the same. The biggest change is that Menifee County rises from 1 to 18 targets in the top 120 (Frenchburg quadrangle from 1 to 14). These crossings are geologically comparable (verified Mbrn top, good relief and source supply), sit only 6–12 km outside the outline, and are not documented. They are the best "frontier" test sites, for example:

  • 37.8838, -83.6976 — Camp Branch (Frenchburg, Menifee)
  • 37.9217, -83.6682 — Indian Creek (Frenchburg, Menifee)
  • 37.8956, -83.7430 — Right Fork Cane Creek (Frenchburg, Menifee)

The Irvine-centred history (Estill County) is at least partly a matter of access and publicity. The Big Hill and Owsley Fork area ranks as high as or higher than Irvine on geology alone.

Weaker hypotheses, flagged as such: fault-guided fluids along the Locust Branch / Kentucky River trend (the Zn-Pb mineralized Renfro at Rock Lick Creek, Dever section 89) and Big Sinking oil-field fluids may have helped silicification locally. Faults were measured and shown in the app but are not used in scores, because no source ties agate to fault proximity.
